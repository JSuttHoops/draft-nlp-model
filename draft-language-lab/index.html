The model is best used for downside detection. It is not a definitive ranking or a claim that one player is better than another; it is a way to see how repeated scouting language has historically lined up with NBA outcomes.
What is xRAPM? xRAPM is a line-up adjusted impact metric, as part of the Real Adjusted Plus-Minus family of metrics. It estimates how much a player contributes to a team's scoring margin, while adjusting for their teammates and context. Typically, it passes the eye test, though it can sometimes bring in some out-of-left-field conclusions. Regardless, it's one of the most robust all-in-one metrics in the NBA today that doesn't require tracking data.

What this model is for

This model is not to make definitive predictions, nor is it to say that one player is better than the other. It is simply to gain perspective on how what scouts say has potentially impacted what players do in the league.
